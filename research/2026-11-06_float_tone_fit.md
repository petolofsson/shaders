# Bounded monotone tone fit on same-update observations

The user asked to continue until a meaningful decision is needed. This study asks whether a **single, strictly increasing output-luminance curve** can recover some of the measured dark relative response while protecting other materials. Fitting a bounded, slope-limited curve to four captured patches improves selected shelf and metal gradient statistics and approximately preserves the selected cool-window response. It also brightens large areas of unrelated scenes, compresses part of the near-black ramp and amplifies some synthetic noise. The model is a useful feasibility result, **not a production candidate**: it is fitted to two scenes, has no demonstrated universal black anchor or GPU/motion contract, and lacks the requested color solution.

## Fit and constraints

Use the [same-update float pairs](2026-11-06_shadow_float_detail.md) for the shelf, metal, blue-wall and cool-window 64×64 patches. For valid horizontal/vertical four-pixel pairs with post-inverse input Y above 0.001 and input-relative log gaps 0.03–0.5 stops, fit one piecewise-linear correction in **log output Y** to reduce the difference between post-inverse input and pre-dither output log gaps. Give each patch equal weight, add fixed ridge and second-difference regularization, and fix the curve to identity at output Y≤0.00175 and Y≥0.30. Bound correction to ±0.25 stops and its log slope to 0.5–1.5 using a convex projected numerical solve. The threshold 0.00175 is chosen to cover the two observed [black-fixture](2026-11-06_float_black_handoff.md) output luminances; it is not an established black bound for other scenes. The target is reduced input/output contrast difference, not a claim that input is the artistically correct output.

The final projected-gradient residual is 4.62e-14 and the sampled piecewise log slopes are 0.5–1.5. The construction is monotone for scalar positive Y, has no spatial filter and leaves exact output Y=0 unchanged. It does **not** guarantee preserved source-black response outside the tested fixture, RGB gamut after gain, color appearance, dither, noise, or motion. It has no automatic scene adaptation. Output range, colors and target scenes were not calibrated for a production design.

Two predecessor calculations are retained. A fit trained only on shelf/metal made their coarse response better but worsened the cool materials; its unconstrained solution reached about 0.54 stop. Adding cool patches and a fixture-based black anchor produced a 0.082 minimum log slope near black. The current explicit 0.5 lower slope addresses that severe scalar compression, but still permits a 50% relative slope reduction. These are sequential design constraints, not a broad strength sweep. Exact code, coefficients, input data and results are under `captures/shadow_ordered_fit_20261106/`, `captures/shadow_global_cooperative_20261106/` and `captures/shadow_limited_cooperative_20261106/`.

## Selected results

On training patch pairs the input-signed mean output log gaps in stops are 0.10988→0.15834 shelf and 0.10992→0.16185 metal; wall is 0.05868→0.06027 and window 0.14536→0.14710. Their respective post-inverse input gaps are 0.19914, 0.20434, 0.06933 and 0.15851. These overlapping pairs are selected, and training statistics are not independent validation. The larger, spatially different grids from the **same two scenes** show shelf 0.12458→0.17790, metal 0.17735→0.21921, wall 0.08361→0.08620 and window 0.18772→0.18904. These are internal cross-checks, not independent scenes. Existing float readback encoding, zero-dither, excluded overrange samples and static-history limits remain.

Applying the fixed fit as a separate output-only operation to existing 8-bit stills increases source-selected four-pixel log-Y gaps 0.11596→0.16648 shelf and 0.13701→0.16737 metal; wall changes 0.07589→0.07494 and window 0.14997→0.15382. Shelf L median stays 15.735, whereas metal rises 17.589→18.501. These screenshot proxies are not a compiled shader, and their source masks, independent capture and quantization differ from the float training pairs. Native shelf and metal crops show a subtle difference, not established gameplay preference.

## Held scenes and controls

The curve was also applied offline to current-profile GZW daylight, independently verified vivid GZW-profile Arc content, and current Arc emitter baselines. No new GPU captures were made. Across the full screenshots, maximum differences reach 6/6/7 codes and >2-code pixels number 1,727,399/1,480,903/2,217,440 respectively. In GZW daylight, selected foliage median L rises 27.476→28.656 and its L gap falls 5.323→5.097; lit-paving gap falls 17.579→16.782. In vivid, blue-sky gap falls 10.567→9.779. Arc red-lamp median rises 26.005→27.100 and its gap falls 21.180→20.725. These are selected screenshot metrics, not perceptual judgments, and preserve the original baseline provenance and profile epochs. The source-fixed rectangles and complete records are in `held.json`. Arc emitter baseline matches its adoption artifact hash; the vivid baseline matches its separately verified image hash. The daylight baseline retains its documented inverse-snapshot provenance limitation.

A synthetic 0–64-code neutral ramp produces 63 distinct 8-bit output codes, including two repeated adjacent values, with no reversed row steps. For independent ±1-code noise around output code 14, continuous encoded standard deviation rises 0.817→1.179 codes; the discrete rounded result rises to 1.247. This intentionally adverse fixture is not real game noise, a visibility judgment or motion evidence. It shows a concrete risk of fitting local contrast. Exact ramp, other levels and scripts are in `synthetic.json`.

The fitted curve can offer useful selected contrast but spends a substantial portion of its bounded adjustment on broad tonal changes, carries noise, and has not addressed subtle source colors. No 16-bit game swapchain was activated, no compiled candidate, exact-black GPU endpoint, held motion or active-config test was performed. Production shaders, profiles, configs and ratings remain unchanged. Continue from a mechanism that can protect lighting and palette beyond the two fitted scenes rather than requesting adoption of this curve.
