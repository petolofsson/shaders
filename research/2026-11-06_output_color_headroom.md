# Output-color headroom screen

The [output-color research proxy](2026-11-06_ordered_shadow_design.md) blends current output RGB toward grade-input RGB scaled to the same output luminance. The two selected image proxies had no out-of-range channels, but that does not establish a general SDR contract. A deliberately constructed saturated-face case does exceed one before clipping. A shared correction multiplier based on positive channel headroom prevents this on the sampled inputs while retaining ideal luminance. This is a CPU arithmetic result, not a compiled GPU contract or an argument that the color change is visually suitable.

For nonnegative SDR `input` and `current`, let `Y=Luma(current)`, `X=Luma(input)`, and let `target=input*Y/max(X,1e-8)`. The proposed correction is `delta=0.15*gate*(target-current)`, using the two smooth gates from the preceding report. For positive `X` when the gate is active, `Luma(delta)=0` in ideal arithmetic. Nonnegative blending is guaranteed for the original correction because `target` and `current` are nonnegative and the blend weight is at most 0.15, but `target` can exceed one even when both input and current are SDR.

For `current=[0,0.004,1]` and `input=[0,0,1]`, current Y is 0.0750608 and the output-shadow gate is about 0.01423. The ungated-headroom blend produces blue approximately 1.0000846. This deliberately constructed case shows that random samples and the two screenshots alone cannot prove the bound. Applying one common factor `alpha=min(1, min_i((1-current_i)/delta_i for delta_i>0))` to all channels of `delta` keeps positive increments within available headroom; the factor is zero at an already-saturated channel requesting a positive increment. The correction remains zero-luma in exact arithmetic because it is scaled uniformly. The minimum makes the factor continuous but can change derivative where the limiting channel changes; that and transitions around zero increment need their own continuity checks. Existing signed or positive-overrange internal signals are not covered by this SDR-only construction.

The deterministic CPU experiment uses 400,000 independent RGB pairs, including 40,000 randomly chosen single-channel face values in each sample population and the explicit case above. Of 111,908 pairs with nonzero gate, one exceeded one by more than 1e-12 before the safeguard and none afterward; no sampled result was negative. Maximum safeguarded luma difference was 2.78e-17 in double precision. Independent pairs deliberately include combinations that may not occur in the real chain and are not a representative image distribution. The explicit face case is included in the total, and the sample does not prove every finite RGB or shader rounding case. Exact generation and results: `captures/shadow_output_color_contract_20261106/`.

The bounded proxy still has the preceding GZW source-neutral shift, subtle gains, cool-material loss and synthetic quantization sensitivity. A headroom fix does not resolve those visual issues. This screen changes no shader, profile, config or rating. If a visually worthwhile design emerges, it still requires a direct GPU numeric test, signed/overrange policy, boundary ramps, black handoff, real warm/red-black and muted cases, measured motion, and actual-config validation.
