# Tree-line band under skylines — 2026-11-11

User report (GZW overcast, screenshots 210137 shader on / 210201 off): with the shader on, the
top of the tree line reads as a noticeable deep-green band; wanted an unobtrusive fade into sky.

## Measurement (210201 through the GZW profile, headless, settled)

Rows 0–50 px below the ridge (three columns) vs the hill body below (rows 850–1000):

| Variant | band RGB | band luma / body | band G−R |
|---|---|---|---|
| source | 29 41 43 | .649 | 12.6 |
| live | 22 41 43 | .578 | 18.9 |
| surround tint 0 | 30 42 43 | .637 | 12.6 |
| zone / lift / CC / neutralize / tonal / chroma / inverse off | ≈ live | .578–.582 | 17.0–18.9 |
| fix (vertical blur below only) | 29 42 43 | .635 | 12.8 |

Cause: shadow surround tint. The overcast sky (Oklab L ≈ .4) passes the surface window
(.10–.80), so shade within one environment-blur radius (~55 px) under the skyline reads 1–2 stops
darker than its surroundings, takes the sky's cyan hue and the lightness-preserving darkening.
Shade further down sees only hill and gets nothing, so a band forms.

## First fix (superseded, see revision)

The vertical environment blur uses only the pixel and taps below it: bounce reaches shade from
surfaces beside and below; what lies above is mostly sky, whose light is already in the render.
Horizontal pass unchanged. 10 fewer taps in the vertical pass. Tested via a weight hook (0 and .25;
.25 left G−R 14.9), then hard-coded as a one-sided loop: bit-identical to the hook at 0 on 210201
and 172413.

Side effects vs live (max / px > 2 codes): 172413 21 / 8.9%, 133932 35 / 18.2%, 133729 12 / 8.3%;
mean luma within .1%. Changes concentrate on skylines (172413 ridge, 133729 tree line). 133932:
shade under the bright haze keeps slightly less surround tint, no visible harm. Not live-played.

## Revision: soft taper (user, live play)

transition from dark to lighter green. Taps above the pixel now use a weaker, wider triangle
(weight .40·(1 − |d|/15), reach 14 texels at 1/16 ≈ 225 px) instead of the Gaussian (r 6) or
